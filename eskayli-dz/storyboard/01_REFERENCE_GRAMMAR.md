## 1. Measured rhythm
- **Opener (A, 0–31 s)**: music at **125 BPM**; one word per beat (0.48–0.5 s), then two-word features held for 2 s ("CUTS RAPIDES", "COULEUR CORRECTION", …). No narration: the music carries the pace.
- **Pattern break (A, 14.0–15.0 s)**: one full second of black *and* silence (−50 dB) before the feature list. The silence resets attention.
- **Shot length (A, whole film)**: 76 detected cuts, median shot **3.0 s**, mean 3.85 s, p10 0.2 s, p90 7.2 s — very fast bursts alternating with long talking-head holds on which overlays change every 1–2 s.
- **Reel (B)**: a new visual event every 0.5–1 s; one idea per frame; entrances on the beat.

## 2. Typography behaviour
- **One word, then the next** — words enter on the beat, centred, relatively small in a lot of negative space.
- **Two-beat reveal** — the second word of a pair first appears ghosted (lighter, blurred), then solidifies on the next beat ("CUTS › RAPIDES", "A video › editor"). It creates anticipation without motion noise.
- **Mixed-scale hierarchy** — a small connector line above a large keyword ("Ton / **équipe**", "Tu as pensé / **Recruter**", "Tu ressens / **une frustration**"). The keyword carries the idea; the connector carries the grammar.
- **Accent on one word only** — a colour, a script or an italic serif marks the emotional word; everything else stays neutral.

## 3. Composition and information design
- **One dominant idea per frame**, centred; background changes mark sentence boundaries (light gradient ↔ deep colour ↔ grey).
- **UI cards as narrative devices** — a small alert card ("⚠ Le vrai problème c'est…"), app pills with status dots, stat cards, profile cards, a checklist pill ("✓ Oui"). The UI *says* the line instead of illustrating it.
- **Orbit / system diagrams** — a central node (a person, a business) with satellite icons on a dashed circle; later the satellites detach ("Tu plafonnes toujours"). A system drawn, not described.
- **Icon + label lists** that build one line at a time next to the subject.
- **Depth**: the background is defocused when text or a card takes the foreground (rack focus), then snaps back.

## 4. Motion language
- Entrances: slam with directional blur, pop with a small overshoot, ghost-to-solid fades, lateral slides.
- Transitions: colour-band wipes, white flash cuts, blur dissolves, zoom-through, star/shape masks (B).
- Camera: slow push-ins on holds; punch-ins on emphasis; lateral tracking across UI.
- Tension → release: fast word bursts → a held frame or a black beat → a clean reveal.

## 5. Sound rhythm
- Music-forward, tempo-locked edit; a hit or tick on each word; risers into reveals; one full silence as a pattern break; dense, loud master (A: ~−9 dB RMS).
- Narration, when present, takes over the pace and the music steps back.

## What Eskayli takes, transforms and refuses

| Grammar | In the references | In Eskayli |
|---|---|---|
| Word-per-beat hook | neon italic caps on colour fields | Geist 800, off-white on near-black, words land on the beat of a 100 BPM pulse; one accent word per line |
| Two-beat reveal | ghost → solid | kept: « Votre BUDGET » ghost → « PUBLICITAIRE » solid; used for pairs and questions |
| Mixed-scale hierarchy | small connector + big keyword | kept for subtitles: small grey connectors, large white keywords, one orange concept |
| UI cards as narrative | pills, alert cards, stat cards | a fictional **campaign manager** in French (no official Meta UI), audience profiles, test cards, a conversation, a pipeline — the UI *is* the story |
| Orbit diagram | person + icons on a dashed circle | the **business core** with its six satellites (offre, marché, client idéal, besoins, frustrations, motivations) that lock into a system |
| Pattern break | 1 s of black and silence | « L'objectif ? » — the frame empties and the music drops out for one beat |
| Background alternation | light ↔ blue ↔ grey | noise world (grain, scattered, dim) ↔ system world (hairline grid, precise, accent flows) ↔ one off-white brand frame |
| Rack focus | defocus behind cards | depth layers with blur on the far plane; the camera "racks" when a new layer becomes the subject |
| Neon glow, frame borders, heavy italic caps, gamer colours | — | **refused**: the brief forbids neon, cyberpunk and glowing blue dashboards |
| Talking head, B-roll of people, stock-like stills | — | **refused**: interface-driven storytelling, silhouettes and abstract identities instead of faces |

**Eskayli's own grammar in one line**: *a signal becomes a system becomes a conversion* — one accent-coloured signal (the ad budget) travels, unbroken, through every chapter: it enters a campaign, is routed by strategy, split into tests, narrowed by optimisation and ends as a client.
